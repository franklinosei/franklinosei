## A little about me

I'm a Ghanaian Software Engineer who enjoys figuring out problems and building solutions that work.

I don't believe in being tied to a particular language, framework, or tool. **I'm tool-agnostic: I learn and choose the technology that best fits the problem at hand.** Sometimes that means using a familiar stack; other times it means learning something new, understanding its trade-offs, and putting it to work.

My experience spans software development, data-intensive applications, APIs, ETL pipelines, cloud infrastructure, and AI-enabled solutions. At the World Food Programme, I contribute to digital systems supporting food security analysis and early warning.

I'm interested in the engineering behind useful software: good architecture, maintainable code, reliable infrastructure, thoughtful automation, and systems that balance quality with real-world constraints.

I want to keep growing as an engineer who can move from understanding a problem to designing and delivering a practical solution—especially solutions that can make a difference for businesses and communities across Africa.

## How I approach engineering

- **Problem first, tools second.** Choose technology based on the requirements, constraints, and trade-offs—not hype.
- **Understand the whole system.** Think beyond code to architecture, data, infrastructure, security, deployment, and operations.
- **Keep learning.** Stay curious, explore unfamiliar tools, and build the knowledge needed to solve the next problem.
- **Build for the real world.** Value maintainability, reliability, usability, and cost as much as technical elegance.
- **Use AI purposefully.** Explore ways to apply AI and automation where they create real value.
